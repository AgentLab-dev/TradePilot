# Emotional Writing — Before/After Examples & Templates

Reference for `emotional-writing`. Each pair shows the same content with and without emotional intelligence.

## Principle

Decide the feeling the reader should leave with, acknowledge their reality first, then deliver. Sincere and specific — never manipulative.

---

## Delivering Hard News

**Before (cold, defensive):**
> "The deployment is delayed due to issues found in testing. We'll update when it's resolved."

**After (honest + respectful + path):**
> "I want to be straight with you: testing surfaced a data issue, so we're holding the deployment to avoid a bad release. Here's the plan — fix today, re-validate tomorrow, deploy Thursday. I'll update you by end of day each day until it's out."

Target feeling: *informed and reassured*, not anxious.

---

## Responding to Pushback / Conflict

**Before (escalates):**
> "That's not correct. The process clearly states the AE team owns this."

**After (de-escalate + redirect):**
> "I don't think we're far apart here. I want to make sure I understand your concern before we land this — can you walk me through how you're seeing ownership? Here's how I've understood it, and where I think we can align."

Target feeling: *respected and heard*, so they stay open.

---

## Apology That Rebuilds Trust

**Before (hollow):**
> "Sorry for any inconvenience caused."

**After (specific + accountable + forward):**
> "I dropped the ball on getting you the numbers Friday, and I know that pushed your prep into the weekend. That's on me. Here they are now, and I've set a reminder so the Thursday hand-off is locked going forward."

Target feeling: *taken seriously*; trust restored.

---

## Motivating Someone

**Before (generic):**
> "Great job, keep it up."

**After (specific + meaning):**
> "The way you traced that variance to the source bug saved us a bad close — that's exactly the judgment this team runs on. The next phase needs that same instinct, and I want you leading it."

Target feeling: *valued and energized*.

---

## Recognition (public)

**Before:** "Thanks to the team for the hard work."

**After:** "Two call-outs: A stayed late so three downstream teams weren't blocked Monday, and B caught a filter bug that would've dropped six months of data. This release is clean because of them."

Target feeling: *seen*; signals to others what excellence looks like.

---

## Asking for Something (a favor / extra effort)

**Before:** "Can you get this done by tomorrow?"

**After:** "I know your plate is full this week, so I don't ask lightly — I need [X] by tomorrow because [reason that matters to them too]. If that's not doable, tell me and we'll find another way."

Target feeling: *respected, not dumped on*.

---

## Sensitive Message Template

```
[Acknowledge their reality / feeling — 1 line]
[The honest core message — clear, no hedging maze]
[Why it matters / shared value — 1-2 lines]
[A concrete next step they can take — restores agency]
[Warm, confident close]
```

---

## Calibration Notes

- **Warm up** when morale is low, trust is thin, you're asking for extra, or delivering bad news.
- **Cool down** when emotions are already hot or you need to look composed and senior. In heated moments, less emotion in the words projects more control.

## Final Editing Pass

- [ ] Read once as the recipient on a bad day — does any line sting needlessly?
- [ ] Acknowledged before I asked?
- [ ] Does the last line achieve the target feeling?
- [ ] Cut hedging ("just", "I think maybe") that dilutes sincerity.
- [ ] Ends on something the reader can do or feel good about?
