# Political Resilience — Worked Scenarios & Comeback Scripts

Reference for `political-resilience-mentor`. Use these as patterns, not scripts to paste blindly — adapt to the real names, facts, and relationship.

## Principle Behind Every Script

Calm + facts + forward. Never defend emotionally in the moment; respond on record where you control the frame. The composed person with receipts wins.

---

## Scenario 1 — Asked to own work/risk you didn't create

**Situation:** Another team built something and wants you to own it in production.

**Bad (emotional, defensive):**
> "This isn't fair — we never agreed to this and I shouldn't have to support code I didn't write."

**Good (hand the decision up, on record):**
> "Happy to help get this over the line. One thing to confirm before we deploy: since [team] developed this and we weren't part of the requirements or design, who owns production support if it breaks? Can you confirm the owning team so we're aligned before promotion?"

**Why it works:** You look collaborative, the accountability question is now in writing, and the decision sits with management — not you.

---

## Scenario 2 — Blamed for a shared or someone else's failure

**Bad:** "That wasn't me — that was [name]'s mistake." (sounds defensive, throws a peer under the bus)

**Good (facts + timeline, no emotion):**
> "Let me lay out the timeline so we can find the real fix. On [date] we agreed X; the change that caused this came in on [date] from [source]. Here's what I'd suggest to prevent it next time."

**Why it works:** You move the room from blame to resolution, and the record quietly clears you without an attack.

---

## Scenario 3 — Your idea gets hijacked

**Bad:** Stew silently, or "Actually that was MY idea."

**Good (reclaim forward):**
> "Glad this is landing — building on the approach I proposed last week, the natural next step is [X]. I can take that."

**Why it works:** You re-anchor authorship as fact, claim the next move, and look generous instead of territorial.

---

## Scenario 4 — Ambushed in a meeting

**In the room (do NOT defend under fire):**
> "That's a fair challenge — let me pull the specifics and follow up so I give you an accurate answer."

**After (in writing, where you control the frame):**
> "Following up on [topic] from today's call. Here's the data: [facts]. Based on this, I'd recommend [X]. Happy to discuss."

**Why it works:** You deny them the live reaction, stay composed (= senior), and win on record.

---

## Scenario 5 — Vague pressure to "just get it done"

**Good (convert ambiguity to explicit owners):**
> "Want to make sure I execute this right. Confirming: scope is [X], owner for [decision] is [name], and the deadline is [date]. If that's right, I'll proceed."

**Why it works:** Ambiguity is where blame breeds. You force clarity politely and create a record of what was actually agreed.

---

## Scenario 6 — Excluded from a decision that affects you

**Good (one clean question, on record):**
> "Catching up on [decision] — want to make sure I'm aligned. Who's the owner, and what was decided? I'll adjust on my side accordingly."

**Why it works:** Surfaces the exclusion without complaint, and signals you track these things.

## The "Playing It Back" Move — Full Walkthrough

When a power play opens a door (a pilot, a working group, a review), turn it into your guardrail.

1. **Absorb calmly** — no reaction in the heat.
2. **Document the facts** within hours, neutrally.
3. **Reframe to shared interest** — tie your position to audit/stability/delivery, not preference.
4. **Hand the decision to its owner**, in writing, with a deadline.
5. **Shape the opening** — make *your* conditions (ownership defined, incident RACI, review gate) the entry criteria of the pilot.
6. **Bank an ally** — thank someone publicly, make them look good.

Result: the thing moves forward *on your terms*, and you look like the most reasonable person in the room.

---

## On-Record vs. Off-Record

| Channel | Use for |
|---------|---------|
| In writing (email/Slack) | Decisions, ownership, agreements, anything you may need later |
| Live / voice | Emotion, repair, persuasion, anything you don't want quoted |

Rule: **negotiate live, confirm in writing.** "Great chat — confirming what we agreed: …"
