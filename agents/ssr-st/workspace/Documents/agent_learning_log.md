### 2026-08-09 — Realized vol is not implied vol, and the gap between them IS the trade  [INSIGHT / METHOD]
- **What happened:** To avoid pulling 36 option chains, `screen_debit.py` priced synthetic spreads off each name's **realized** vol marked up 15% for the volatility risk premium. Reasonable shortcut, wrong in both directions and badly wrong for one name. **PLTR: synthetic IV 106% (realized 92%) vs actual quoted IV 52.6%.** The synthetic screen scored PLTR at **−6% expectancy and would have rejected it**; the real chain scores **+27% and it is a top-three ticket.** NOW went the other way (synthetic 72% vs real 57.9%).
- **The actual insight, which is bigger than the bug:** PLTR's options are priced for roughly **half the movement the stock actually delivers**. That is not noise — it is exactly the condition the IV-routing matrix says to buy, and it is a stronger reason to own the trade than any momentum reading I had. **The implied-minus-realized spread should be a ranking input, not a byproduct.**
- **Rule / fix:**
  1. Synthetic screens are for **generating candidates only**. No name is ranked, sized, or rejected on a synthetic price.
  2. **Compute implied − realized for every finalist and report it.** Buying when implied sits well below realized is the edge; selling when it sits well above is the income book's edge. Same number, both directions.
- **Status:** adopted.

### 2026-08-09 — Endpoint backtest ≠ live P&L; a 0% breach rate can mean "no adverse regime in the sample"  [MISS / CODE FIX]

### 2026-08-10 — "Stock is up so IV must be worth selling" is backwards for puts  [RULE]
- **What happened:** Considered put credit spreads on PLTR / MPC / NEM on the theory that a
  gap-up made put premium rich. The tape said the exact opposite. From Friday's close to
  Monday 8:30 PT, with all three stocks up 2–5%:
  | | Fri close | Mon | change |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PLTR 155p | $4.53 | $3.25 | −28% |
  | MPC 280p | $7.55 | $4.20 | −44% |
  | NEM 105p | $3.01 | $2.24 | −26% |
- **Why:** equity vol is inversely correlated with price. A rally drops put value through
  *both* channels at once — delta moves the strike further OTM, and the vol surface drops.
  The rich day to sell puts is the day the stock falls, not the day it rallies.
- **Rule / fix:** Never infer premium richness from price direction. Compare the contract's
  IV against its own trailing IV and against realized vol before calling anything "worth
  selling". Selling puts into a gap-up is chasing premium that has already deflated — the
  mirror image of the anti-chase rule on the buy side.
- **Second finding:** a 10% buffer is not "safe" on a high-vol name. Path-aware, PLTR's $160
  short strike (−10.3%) closed below only 12% of the time but *traded through* 48% of the
  time in six weeks. Endpoint safety and live safety are different numbers, and the stop
  fires on the second one.
- **Status:** adopted. Only NEM 110/100p cleared on expectancy (+5% on risk); MPC rejected on
